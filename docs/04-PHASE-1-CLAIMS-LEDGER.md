It is deliberately conservative. Subsequent phases expanded the research substantially; Phase 5 preserves these foundation claims in the canonical claim registry.

## Evidence notes

### FND-001 — frequency

NIST time-and-frequency references define frequency and wavelength conventions and use hertz as cycles per second.

Source:
- https://www.nist.gov/pml/time-and-frequency-division

### FND-002 — frequency is not identity

This is a synthesis rather than a quote from one source.

Evidence is the coexistence of mature theories in which different degrees of freedom carry waves:

- acoustics: pressure/density/displacement;
- electrodynamics: electromagnetic fields;
- condensed matter: lattice normal modes;
- quantum mechanics: matter-wave amplitudes;
- general relativity: gravitational radiation.

The claim is intentionally modest: **the number in hertz does not determine which theory or degree of freedom produced it.**

### FND-003 — sound

NIST describes sound waves in air as alternate compression and expansion that changes gas density.

Source:
- https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2022/06/now-hear

### FND-004 / FND-005 — electromagnetic waves

Maxwell's equations produce the electromagnetic wave equation. Spectrum-region names classify portions of that same electromagnetic phenomenon.

Sources:
- NIST Journal of Research: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/jres/100/4/j14lym.pdf
- Particle Data Group electromagnetic relations index: https://pdg.lbl.gov/

### FND-006 — phonons

The condensed-matter literature experimentally measures and theoretically calculates full phonon dispersion relations. Phonons describe collective lattice vibrational modes.

Sources:
- Wei & Chou, PRL 69, 2799 (1992): https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.69.2799
- Holt et al., PRL 83, 3317 (1999): https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.83.3317
- Maldovan, Nature 503, 209–217 (2013): https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12608

### FND-007 / FND-008 — molecular spectroscopy

NIST maintains critically reviewed molecular rotational spectral databases, including microwave-region data. Terahertz/infrared spectroscopy probes low-frequency and internal molecular vibrational dynamics.

Sources:
- NIST Molecular Spectroscopic Data: https://www.nist.gov/pml/molecular-spectroscopic-data
- Banks et al., Nature Reviews Chemistry 7, 480–495 (2023): https://doi.org/10.1038/s41570-023-00487-w

### FND-009 — matter waves

Davisson and Germer observed diffraction of electrons from nickel crystals, a foundational experimental demonstration of electron wave behavior.

Source:
- https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRev.30.705

### FND-010 / FND-015 — gravitational-wave spectrum

Gravitational-wave astronomy uses multiple frequency bands and detector classes. Reviews explicitly distinguish the gravitational-wave spectrum from the electromagnetic spectrum.

Sources:
- Sathyaprakash & Schutz: https://doi.org/10.12942/lrr-2009-2
- Gair et al.: https://doi.org/10.12942/lrr-2013-7
- Aggarwal et al.: https://doi.org/10.1007/s41114-021-00032-5

### FND-011 — heat

Phononics literature treats mechanical vibration as central to both sound and thermal transport in solids, while blackbody radiation is electromagnetic.

Sources:
- IUPAC Gold Book, *heat*: https://doi.org/10.1351/goldbook.H02752
- NIST, *Heat Transfer*: https://www.nist.gov/glossary-term/24981
- Maldovan, Nature 503, 209–217 (2013): https://doi.org/10.1038/nature12608
- NIST blackbody / Planck-law metrology material: https://www.nist.gov/system/files/documents/iaao/SIM-Metrology-School-Optical-Metrology-Y-Ohno-2.pdf

### FND-012 — energy and frequency

The relation `E = hf` is foundational for photons/quantized modes and quantum transitions. It should not be generalized into "all classical energy equals frequency times Planck's constant."

Reference hubs:
- IUPAC Gold Book, *energy* (`E = hν` for photons): https://doi.org/10.1351/goldbook.E02101
- NIST fundamental constants: https://physics.nist.gov/cuu/Constants/
- Particle Data Group: https://pdg.lbl.gov/

### FND-013 — quantum fields

Quantum field theory underlies the Standard Model description of elementary particles.

Sources:
- Particle Data Group: https://pdg.lbl.gov/
- Hollik, CERN lecture, *Quantum field theory and the Standard Model*: https://doi.org/10.5170/CERN-2010-002.1
- CERN Higgs/QFT explainer: https://home.cern/science/physics/higgs-boson/what/

### FND-014 — dark matter and oscillatory candidates

Dark matter's microscopic nature remains unknown. Specific ultralight candidate models can produce oscillatory signals or behave as coherent fields and are searched experimentally.

Sources:
- Wcisło et al., Nature Astronomy 1, 0009 (2017): https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-016-0009
- Cervantes et al., PRL 129, 201301 (2022): https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.129.201301
- Differential atom interferometry / ultralight dark matter, Nature (2026): https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10617-1

## Phase 1 conclusion

The scientific premise that survives Phase 1 is narrower — and stronger — than "everything is vibration":

> **Many physically distinct systems admit meaningful spectral or oscillatory descriptions. Frequency provides a common coordinate across those systems, while the underlying degree of freedom tells us what the phenomenon actually is.**

That is the premise the rest of the project will test and expand.
